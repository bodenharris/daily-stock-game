# Decisions

Choices made while building, with the reason for each. Newest at the bottom of each section.

## Setup

- **Tooling via `npx sv add`.** Drizzle (postgres.js), Better Auth (email + password, anonymous plugin), `adapter-node`, ESLint, Prettier and Vitest were added with the Svelte CLI as the team asked. The demo routes it generated were deleted.
- **Migrations only, never `push`.** `drizzle/0000_init.sql` is generated by `drizzle-kit generate`; `drizzle/0001_timescale.sql` is hand-written (`drizzle-kit generate --custom`) for the hypertables, columnstore compression, policies and the continuous aggregate. Apply with `npm run db:migrate`.
- **Continuous aggregates confirmed on our plan.** Before building on them, a hypertable, columnstore compression and a real-time continuous aggregate were created inside a transaction on the shared database and rolled back. All worked (TimescaleDB 2.32, license `timescale`).
- **`BETTER_AUTH_URL` falls back to `ORIGIN`.** Both are optional-in-practice locally; the auth base URL uses `BETTER_AUTH_URL` when set.
- **Long sessions with a cookie cache.** Sessions last a year (so streaks survive) and the session is cached in a signed cookie for 5 minutes, so most requests never touch the session table.

## Data

- **Split-adjusted prices only**, computed from Tiingo's raw OHLC and `splitFactor`, not Tiingo's dividend-adjusted `adj*` fields, so the prices shown at the reveal look like the quotes people remember (GME closed at $86.88 split-adjusted on 2021-01-27 = $347.51 / 4).
- **Downloads are cached, then loaded.** `scripts/fetch-prices.ts` saves raw Tiingo JSON to `.cache/tiingo/` (gitignored, paced for the free plan's hourly limit, resumable); `scripts/ingest-prices.ts` upserts into the hypertable and compresses old chunks. Fetching and loading are separate so a rate limit never leaves the database half-written.
- **`prices` chunks are one year, segmented by ticker.** A puzzle reads one ticker's window, so segmenting the columnstore by ticker means only that ticker's segment is decompressed.

## Game

- **The server never sends what has not happened yet.** Before the reveal the client gets only the visible bars, normalised so the last visible close is 100, with no dates and no ticker. Each stretch is sent only after its call is locked in a database transaction.
- **Each call gets an equal slot of chart width.** The context takes half the width and the five calls share the other half equally, whatever their length in trading days, so a 1-day call and a 1-month call both read as one clear chunk of future.
- **A dashed level line marks "now".** Up means the line ends above it, down means below. This is the whole tutorial.
- **Players are created lazily.** Opening the page creates nothing. The first call creates an anonymous Better Auth user; signing up later moves their games to the account (the account's game wins if both played the same day).
- **Dev plays never touch the real crowd.** Games and guesses from a dev server are tagged `source = 'dev'`; the public crowd split and stats count only `real`. In development the crowd also includes `dev` and `sim` rows so the payout can be exercised.

## Money (from the simulator, `node scripts/simulate.ts`)

- **"Clearly beats" means what a player feels:** in every one of 30 scenarios (5 chart models × 6 crowd models, 20,000 games each), thoughtful play beats random play on mean, median, log return, share of games above $100 and P10, and beats all-in on all of those except the mean, and finishes higher on more than half of days. The mean is driven by rare huge days, which is not what one daily result feels like.
- **The 50% chip pays 0.8 of the crowd multiplier.** With every chip paying the full multiplier, all-in tied thoughtful play on turning-point charts and had the higher mean in 19 of 30 scenarios. Trimming the biggest chip is the smallest one-sentence rule that breaks "always go max" (thoughtful now wins 30/30, and wins the mean in 24/30) while still paying well when you beat a lopsided crowd (1.6 × 0.8 = 1.28×).
- **Crowd multiplier = 2 × share of other players who called the other way, kept in 0.50×–2.00×.** A 50/50 crowd pays even money, which is the easiest thing to explain. (The original 2.5× cap could never be reached, so it is 2.00×.)
- **A 10-player 50/50 prior** keeps the first players of the day from swinging the multiplier. The multiplier is locked when you call, from the players before you; the split is shown only after your call.
- **Integer cents everywhere**, divisions round down (the locked multiplier itself rounds half up), so every device computes the same result.

## Puzzles

- **30 puzzles from 1987 to 2025, validated against the real data** (`node scripts/check-puzzles.ts`): no flat stretch (< 0.3%), 79 up / 71 down calls overall, no puzzle with all five calls the same, and the last call is not usually "down". "Always call down" wins 47%.
- **Stretch length is per puzzle** (1 day for crashes, up to 21 for bubbles), so the moment plays out across the five calls.
- **#1 is the GameStop squeeze**, the moment most people half-remember.
- **Rejected:** Lehman, Enron, Bear Stearns (not on Tiingo), SVB (trading halted, flat bars; KRE used instead), Archegos/ViacomCBS (bad ticker data), and several all-down or all-up stories.
- **Delisted tickers show their familiar symbol** at the reveal (BBBYQ → BBBY).

## Look

- **Seed `#2E6B3A`, TonalSpot, 2021 spec.** The 2025 spec output of material-color-utilities 0.4 gave a near-black inverse surface and a non-white on-primary in light mode.
- **Down is a harmonised coral, not red,** at lower chroma than the green so Down never shouts louder than Up. Every up/down pair passes AA.
- **Dark mode follows the system.** No toggle, no flash on load.
- **Lucide icons, not Material Symbols:** the icon font is several MB.
- **Self-hosted fonts** (~250 KB): Google Sans Flex for display and numbers, Roboto Flex for body.

## Bonus round

- **Real or Random: three charts a day, the same for everyone,** seeded by the puzzle number. Real charts are random 90-day windows from large-cap tickers (never puzzle tickers or meme stocks). Random charts are GARCH(1,1) walks with fat tails that borrow the volatility of a real window, because a plain random walk is too smooth to fool anyone. Each day has one or two real charts, so "all real" or "all random" never wins outright.
- **The answers stay on the server**; only the first submitted score is stored.

## Stats page

- **Today's per-call crowd split is hidden until you have finished today's game.** Otherwise anyone could look up the split and always take the minority side for the bigger multiplier.
- **Served by a live remote query** (`query.live`) that pushes fresh numbers every 4 seconds, read from the continuous aggregates, with the query time shown on the page.

## After the first playtest and code review

- **The reveal is sequenced, not instant.** The server settles a call immediately, but the dots, balance, result line and next stakes update only after the chart has drawn the stretch (900 ms), and the ending appears only after the last stretch lands. A playtester could read the result from the shrinking stake buttons before the line moved.
- **Green and coral mean one thing: good or bad for you.** Revealed stretches are coloured by whether the call was right, not by direction; the Up and Down buttons are neutral, and the tapped one lights up at once. Before, a winning Down call drew a red line.
- **Straight lines on a log scale.** Smooth curves on 2-bar stretches looked invented; a log price scale keeps the history readable after a 4× spike instead of squashing it flat on every reveal. The size of each move sits under its score dot (the chart slots are too narrow for labels).
- **The result line always explains the crowd payout** ("Right, and only 33% saw it coming. Paid 1.34×." / "Right, but 64% saw it too. Paid 0.82×."). The rule was invisible before.
- **A stored, secret daily schedule.** The first request for a day writes `schedule(number → slug)`; the order is a SHA-256 shuffle keyed by `PUZZLE_SECRET` (falls back to `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`), reshuffled on every pass through the catalog. Day #1 is pinned to GameStop. The public catalog no longer reveals which day plays which chart, and editing the catalog can never change a day that has started.
- **Three hours of grace after midnight.** A game started before midnight Eastern can be finished until 3 a.m.; it is shown before today's chart, and the countdown offers the new chart instead of yanking the result away.
- **Bonus guesses are stored before answers are revealed**, in order, in one atomic update; reloading resumes where you were, and a round can't be replayed. Each chart is real or random on an independent coin flip (earlier answers say nothing about later ones), real windows come only from after US decimalisation (2001), and real and generated charts are rounded identically.
- **Anonymous players are rate-limited per IP (60 an hour).** Generous, because a hackathon hall shares one IP, but a script can no longer mint thousands of players to swing the crowd.
- **Accepted, not fixed:** a determined player can still replay a day by clearing cookies, and a perfect anonymous game can be moved onto an account by signing in. There is no leaderboard to protect, so the cost of preventing it (device fingerprinting, email verification) isn't worth it tonight.
- **`crowd_daily` buckets by UTC day.** Changing it would mean dropping and recreating the aggregate, which the database rules forbid; nothing on screen depends on it yet.
- **No archetype mentions direction**, and the share text is one Wordle-style row (🟩 right, 🟥 wrong, ⭐ right against the crowd) in whole dollars. The three-row stake grid confused the playtester.
- **No global keyboard shortcuts.** Arrow keys used to place irreversible bets from anywhere on the page.
- **Help and account sheets load on first open**, and the stake chips are a native radio group, so the first page doesn't ship the drawer and toggle-group libraries (eager JS on the home page roughly halved).

## After the second playtest (a non-investor)

- **Show the crowd as people, not percentages,** while it is small: "3 of 4 players called down." Over 20 players it switches to a percentage. "You're one of the first" now only appears when you really are first.
- **Prices at the reveal are as quoted on the day.** Prices stay stored split-adjusted (product.md), but a small `splits` table lets the reveal undo later splits, so GameStop peaks at $348 like everyone remembers, not $87.
- **"In hindsight": where the stock was a year later**, read with TimescaleDB's `last()` over the prices hypertable. It gives the name its payoff and costs 0.13 ms.
- **The fifth call gets its own beat** (1.8 s) before the ending takes over; the playtester missed their biggest bet's result.
- **Each settled stretch keeps a faint dashed line at its starting level,** so a V-shaped stretch that ends lower reads as down at a glance.
- **The prompt names the stretch length** ("Up or down over the next 2 trading days?"), so +353% in one call makes sense.
- **Stake chips show their percentage and reset to 25% after every call.** A leftover 50% selection made it easy to bet half the balance by accident.
- **Share never fails silently.** When the clipboard is blocked (some in-app browsers), the text appears ready to select.
- **Small crowds get a place, not a percentile** on the stats page ("3rd of 4 players today"), and the bottom half is told "Tomorrow is another chart."

## Payout, revised after the second playtest

The non-investor playtester read a right call paying less than the stake ("+$21 on $25, paid 0.83×") as the game cheating, and players can't see the odds before calling. The rules were re-tuned under a new constraint: **a right call never pays less than the stake.**

- **A wrong call loses the stake; a right call wins the stake, plus a crowd bonus.** The bonus is (2 × share of other players who called the other way − 1) × 10% of your balance, and zero when you're with the majority. Beating an 80/20 crowd adds 6% of your balance; a 50/50 crowd adds nothing.
- **The bonus is the same dollars whichever chip you used.** It replaces the hidden "50% chip pays 0.8×" trim with one visible sentence, and it is what keeps always-max-stake from beating careful sizing.
- **Chips stay 10/25/50%.** 10/20/40 made all-in stronger in the sweep.
- **Results, 30 scenarios × 20,000 games:** thoughtful play beats random and all-in in 30/30 on median, log return, share of games above $100, P10 and day-to-day wins. Random play's median stays below $100 with a negative log return in all 30.
- **Two goals had to give.** With an even-money floor, a coin-flipper can't lose on average, so random play's mean is $102.45 pooled (at most $107 in any one scenario), and all-in's mean beats thoughtful's in most scenarios. Both are mathematically unavoidable once no win may pay under 1×, and both are things a player can't feel from one game a day; the median, the bust rate (thoughtful 7.5%, all-in 41%) and day-to-day wins all still favour thoughtful play.
- **The result line shows the bonus in dollars** ("Right. 1 of 4 players called up. Crowd bonus +$6.") instead of a multiplier.

## After the second code review

- **Better Auth's public `/api/auth/sign-in/anonymous` route returns 404.** The game creates anonymous players itself, on a first call, behind the per-IP limit; the open route let a script mint sessions around it.
- **The per-IP limit fails open when the address is the proxy's own.** Behind Caddy without `ADDRESS_HEADER`/`XFF_DEPTH`, every visitor looks like 127.0.0.1; locking the whole site out after 60 new players is worse than not limiting. The settings are documented in README and `.env.example` (server config is out of scope tonight).
- **Bonus rounds are seeded with an HMAC of the server secret** (not a constant in the repo), and nothing is generated until a finished game is found; the list of candidate tickers is cached.
- **New stretches draw in neutral ink and take their right/wrong colour only when the last bar lands.** A long stretch that dips and recovers used to be green from the first frame.
- **Accepted limitation: the public catalog plus public price data can identify a chart.** The context bars are a fingerprint, so a determined player with the repo open can look up the answer. Hiding the catalog would mean keeping it out of the public repo the judges read; there is no leaderboard to protect, and recognising a famous moment is part of the fun. Revisit if prizes or rankings are added.

## After the third playtest (a daily-puzzle regular)

- **How to play opens once on a first visit** (remembered in localStorage; if storage is blocked it simply doesn't auto-open). The first prompt also names the premise: a famous market moment, name hidden till the end.
- **The chart gets a scale without a spoiler:** faint gridlines at round % moves from today's level (+50%, −20%…), and a caption, "Last 3 months, daily · then 5 calls". Real prices and dates still appear only at the reveal.
- **The dashed "now" line is the rule, so it is drawn like one:** darker, wider, with small "up" and "down" labels in the next slot.
- **The vertical range only grows during a game.** Each reveal can add room but never re-zooms the history the player already studied.
- **After the fifth call, the controls give way to "Unmasking the chart…"** instead of a greyed-out stake row that looked frozen.
- **Kept on purpose:** the stake resets to 25% after each call (the second playtester bet half by accident with a leftover selection; the third found re-picking mildly annoying; accidental big bets are worse).
- **Rank went into the share text, stake size did not.** Your place today ("3rd of 6 today", or "Beat 64% of players today" for big crowds) rides on the archetype line, read from the percentile sketch when the game finishes. Stake size stays out: the one-row grid tested better than the three-row version that encoded it.

## Data load

- **All 107 tickers are loaded:** 936,332 daily bars from 1980 to today, 47 of 48 yearly chunks compressed, 36 MB → 12 MB.
- **Load prices before launch, not during a day.** Bonus rounds are derived from the set of eligible tickers, so adding tickers changes the bonus charts for any day that hasn't been fully played, including today. Puzzles themselves are unaffected (they are fixed by the stored schedule).

## After the design critique

- **Brand green on the main actions.** Up and Down sit on the primary container colour and the selected stake on primary; the grey-green secondary roles are gone from the play screen.
- **Revealed stretches are drawn in the brand green; right or wrong is a small mark at each stretch's end** (and on the score dots). Colouring the line by right/wrong made a rising stretch red when the call was wrong, which reads as "it fell"; colouring by direction would bring back the two-meanings-of-green problem the first playtester hit.
- **The next stretch is a soft green zone with small drawn arrows; later stretches are faint dotted outlines** instead of grey slabs.
- **Less text on the play screen:** the hint line, "Stake for call N of 5" and the timeframe caption are gone (the pips, the prompt and the first-visit help cover them). At most one gridline each way.
- **The ending card takes the result's colour** (primary container for a profit, a quiet surface for a loss), pops in on a spring, and Share goes full width once the bonus is done. On very short screens the hindsight line is hidden so the card and Share stay above the fold.
- **Scrims at 50%** (32% barely separated a sheet from the page in dark mode).

## Desktop layout
- **Wide screens get their own layout, not a stretched phone.** From 1024px the chart takes the stage on the left in a card of its own, and the game sits in a 360px rail on the right with Up and Down level with the bottom of the chart. The page widens to 1280px, and the chart's height is capped at 700px so it stays landscape on tall monitors. Tablets (768–1023px) keep one column at up to 672px; phones are unchanged.
- **A call log fills the rail on desktop.** Each call shows its direction, bet, how far the price moved and what it paid, instead of the five dots. Phones keep the dots, because they have no room to spare. After the game, the rail shows the dots again so the reveal fits.
- **Keyboard play:** ↑ and ↓ make the call, and 1, 2 and 3 pick the bet. These keys are ignored while typing, inside a sheet or with a radio focused, and the help sheet mentions them on keyboard-and-mouse screens. The buttons show small ↑/↓ key hints on desktop only.
- **Sheets become dialogs from 768px** (M3: bottom sheets for compact windows, dialogs for medium and up). One `Sheet` wrapper keeps the help and account content identical in both.
- **Vibration only on touch screens:** keyboard play on desktop has no tap to unlock it, and it only logged errors.
- **Stats page in two columns on desktop:** today's numbers on the left, the crowd's calls and the 24-hour chart on the right.

## A bigger, private catalog
- **The catalog grew from 30 to 96 puzzles,** so the first repeat is three months away instead of one. Every window was checked against the real prices (no flat stretch, no puzzle with all five calls the same), and every story was fact-checked against news coverage from the time by reviewers who hadn't written it. They corrected 20 stories, for example a record that was the Dow's rather than the S&P's, a "surprise profit" that was really a smaller loss, and a convicted founder who was later pardoned.
- **First calls are balanced.** Many big moments open with the crash itself, so call 1 went down in 50 of the first 80. A player who always opened with "down" would have won 62% of the time. Six windows now start a day earlier, so the event lands on call 2.
- **The catalog is private.** The real catalog (the answers) lives in the shared database and a git-ignored `private/catalog.json`. `scripts/export-catalog.ts` pulls it from the database, `scripts/seed-puzzles.ts` pushes it back, and `scripts/catalog.example.json` shows the format. The first 30 are still in git history; rewriting the history of a shared public repo would hurt the team more than it protects. The judges see the format, the checks and the schedule code, not the answers.
- **Seeding can't quietly retire puzzles.** If the catalog file is missing a puzzle that's in play (a stale or partial file), seeding refuses unless you pass `--retire`. Exporting refuses to overwrite local puzzles the database doesn't have yet.
- **The schedule picks the next unplayed puzzle.** Each day takes, among the puzzles not yet played this pass, the one with the lowest secret-keyed hash. Because it looks only at what has been played, puzzles can be added or retired at any time without a repeat or a skip, and a new pass never opens with the puzzle that closed the last one. The old positional helpers in `daily.ts` were unused and are gone.
- **15 new tickers** (Chegg, Nikola, Tilray, Grayscale Bitcoin Trust, MicroStrategy, Palantir, Carvana, Trump Media, Alibaba, PayPal, Southwest, Coinbase, BlackBerry, Virgin Galactic, Robinhood) were fetched and loaded, which also widens the bonus round's pool.
